Unit I: Propositional Logic

Propositional Logic Basics

Core concepts of propositions and logical connectives.

  • Identifying valid propositions
  • Translating English statements to symbolic forms
  • Using logical connectives like AND, OR, XOR, and NOT
While Propositional Logic Basics gives us the theoretical background, Conditional & Biconditional Statements provides the next layer of complexity.

Conditional & Biconditional Statements

Understanding if-then and if-and-only-if statements.

  • Defining conditional and biconditional statements
  • Determining the truth values of conditional propositions
  • Finding the converse, inverse, and contrapositive
Moving forward from Conditional & Biconditional Statements, let's break down the mechanics of Truth Tables & Proposition Types.

Truth Tables & Proposition Types

Evaluating propositions using exhaustive truth assignments.

  • Constructing truth tables for complex propositions
  • Identifying Tautologies (always true)
  • Identifying Contradictions (always false) and Contingencies
Transitioning from Truth Tables & Proposition Types, we now examine Logical Equivalences, which builds upon these foundational rules.

Logical Equivalences

Proving that different logical statements mean the same thing.

  • Proving equivalence using truth tables
  • Applying De Morgan's laws and distributive laws
  • Simplifying expressions using logical laws (without truth tables)
Now that we have a solid grasp of Logical Equivalences, the natural next step is to explore Logic Applications.

Logic Applications

Applying logic to practical scenarios.

  • Modeling system specifications
  • Expressing constraints using connectives and negations

Unit II: Combinatorics & Recurrence Relations

Permutations & Combinations

Counting the number of ways to arrange or select items.

  • Calculating standard nPr and nCr values
  • Solving algebraic equations involving permutations
  • Forming words and selecting committees
Moving forward from Permutations & Combinations, let's break down the mechanics of Recurrence Relations Basics.

Recurrence Relations Basics

Defining sequences recursively.

  • Finding the order and degree of recurrence relations
  • Calculating subsequent terms given initial conditions
  • Checking if a given sequence is a solution
Having established the core principles of Recurrence Relations Basics, we now turn our attention to Solving Linear Homogeneous Relations.

Solving Linear Homogeneous Relations

Finding closed-form solutions for recurrence relations.

  • Writing the characteristic equation
  • Solving second-order relations with distinct and repeated roots
  • Solving third-order relations with multiple initial conditions
Transitioning from Solving Linear Homogeneous Relations, we now examine Generating Functions, which builds upon these foundational rules.

Generating Functions

Using formal power series to represent sequences and solve recurrences.

  • Finding generating functions for simple sequences
  • Solving recurrence relations by converting to generating functions
  • Applying partial fraction decomposition

Unit III: Graph Theory

Graph Fundamentals

Basic terminology and structure of graphs.

  • Identifying vertices, edges, loops, and parallel edges
  • Calculating degrees, in-degrees, and out-degrees
  • Identifying isolated and pendant vertices
  • Classifying multigraphs and directed pseudographs
Having established the core principles of Graph Fundamentals, we now turn our attention to Special Graphs & Properties.

Special Graphs & Properties

Recognizing standard graph families.

  • Defining and drawing Complete Graphs (Kn)
  • Defining and drawing Complete Bipartite Graphs (Km,n)
  • Understanding Cycle graphs (Cn) and n-Cubes (Qn)
  • Checking whether a graph is bipartite

The Handshaking Theorem

Relating vertex degrees to the number of edges.

  • Applying the degree sum formula (Sum of degrees = 2 * Edges)
  • Solving for unknown degrees or vertices
  • Validating if a graph can exist with given degree sequences
While The Handshaking Theorem gives us the theoretical background, Matrix Representation provides the next layer of complexity.

Matrix Representation

Representing graph connections using matrices.

  • Constructing Adjacency matrices for directed and undirected graphs
  • Constructing Incidence matrices
  • Drawing graphs from a given matrix
While Matrix Representation gives us the theoretical background, Graph Isomorphism provides the next layer of complexity.

Graph Isomorphism

Determining if two graphs are structurally identical.

  • Checking vertex counts, edge counts, and degree sequences
  • Mapping vertices between two potentially isomorphic graphs
